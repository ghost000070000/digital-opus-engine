# Phase 044 characterization — Dynamic utility, not product physics

Phase 044 tested whether a gravity-driven, mutable-velocity Body could be a credible no-code feature without implementing a general rigid-body solver. The candidate reused Phase 043's Static collision service through a qualification-only Kinematic adapter. Its analytic constant-acceleration step was frame-partition consistent in free fall; blocked velocity axes zeroed without suppressing tangential travel. Static Box/Circle response, thin obstacles, rotated geometry, 60-second rest, Trigger ordering, and parent conversion were tested. The prototype did not change any canonical Body data, editor code, export path, or engine bundle.

The isolated utility artifacts are `tools/phase-044-dynamic-prototype.js`, `tests/phase-044-dynamic-body-utility.test.js`, `tools/audit-phase-044-dynamic-fuzz.js`, `tools/audit-phase-044-dynamic-performance.js`, and `tools/audit-phase-044-browser.js`. The real-browser review loaded the existing editor and injected only the qualification script. Twenty cycles confirmed both narrow mechanical behavior and the unaltered product UI (Static/Kinematic choices, no Gravity control).

The prototype demonstrated two substantially simpler no-code workflows: falling/resting objects and ballistic/arcing motion. Falling Box versus Circle, falling Trigger hazard versus debris, and horizontal versus vertical gravity are variants rather than independent third workflows. That misses the required three-workflow value threshold. Lack of Dynamic↔Dynamic, Dynamic↔Kinematic, and Tilemap collision would also conflict with a reasonable creator interpretation of “Dynamic Body.” Because this is a utility/clarity rejection rather than a mechanical failure, the conditional 30–60-minute Dynamic soak was not run. The product Body component remains v1 Static/Kinematic; Phase 045 Character collision can build on Kinematic independently.

Phase 043's 20 historical capped sweeps were recorded only as an aggregate and cannot be individually reconstructed. Phase 044's 500,595-case high-speed fuzz captured 6,065 reproducible fail-closed cap events, including shape pairs, velocities, gravity, deltas, accepted motion, and final gaps. They were concentrated in high-speed/grazing shapes; none occurred in 240,000 resting-contact ticks. This supports safe truncation and no rest-cap storm in the qualified prototype, while preserving the numerical limitation as explicit future friction.

Decision: `UTILITY_GATE_DECISION: NOT_JUSTIFIED`. Final verdict: `PHASE_044_DYNAMIC_BODY_NOT_JUSTIFIED`, subject to final repository regression and packaging checks recorded in `PHASE-044-REPORT.md`.
